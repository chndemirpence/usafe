# Connect voluntary usability pilot

Status: protocol ready; no participants recruited and no field results claimed.

## Invitation and consent

Explain in the participant's chosen language: we are checking whether Connect's
steps and recovery guidance are understandable. Participation is optional,
unpaid unless separately arranged, and may stop at any time. Use a test task,
not a personal account or sensitive browsing destination. Do not ask for IPs,
bridge addresses, browsing history, credentials or screen recordings.

The app does not upload feedback. A participant may review and save its summary
and decide separately whether to share it with the facilitator. The summary is
minimized, not guaranteed anonymous: it reveals app use and connection outcomes.
Declining to share does not prevent using the app.

Before collecting any shared files, identify the data custodian and contact
channel, explain retention (proposed: delete raw files within 30 days after
aggregation), and agree a deletion request process. Keep contact information
separate from results. No raw participant reports go in a public source repo.

## Tasks

1. With a clean ordinary-network setup, find Connect and establish a verified
   session without help. Record completion and elapsed time locally.
2. Open the managed Tor Browser and visit a neutral test page chosen in advance.
3. Ask which browser/apps the connection covers. Record correct/needs-help only.
4. In a controlled test fixture, simulate an unreachable bridge. Ask the person
   to recover using guidance. Do not alter their real network security settings.
5. Ask them to find offline help without opening a website.
6. Offer the local summary preview and fixed-choice feedback. Sharing is optional.

Compare with current Tor Browser using equivalent tasks. Alternate task order
between participants. Record app versions and test setup in facilitator records,
not exact participant location or network identifiers. A lab error simulation is
not evidence of defeating a national censorship system.

## Field pilot gate

A trusted local partner, voluntary informed consent, and context-specific risk
review are prerequisites to a restrictive-network pilot. Evaluate whether any
participation or installation creates extra risk. Remote facilitation must not
collect IPs or bridge details through a screen share. Existing seven-user USAFE
adoption is separate from Connect testing until those tests actually happen.
